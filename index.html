CAT / ABLATION

Memory carries much of the adaptation.

Removing associative memory reduced reversal accuracy from 100% to 61%. Freezing actor learning also failed one of three lives: both components matter in this sample.

RHINO / RETENTION

The unrelated skill survived.

All three complete brains retained the stable odour pair at every measured probe. Their baseline and later stable-skill scores were all 1.00.

Why the absolute score matters

A no-memory life improved from 0.125 to 0.375. Its clipped relative-retention ratio is 1.00, even though its skill remains poor. The original ≥95% absolute floor prevents a false pass.

17.6%FEWER COUNTED SWEEPS

The full denominator matters. The complete brain used 91.3% fewer learning sweeps than the always-learning arm, but 17.6% fewer total counted sweeps after acting and probes were included. Routine still took approximately 32 sweeps per action. Physical energy was not measured.

02 / Claim boundaries

Keep the result in scope.

Supported here

A working, bounded system

  • Qualified neural answers and tested refusal/rollback contracts.
  • Acquisition, reversal, return and stable-skill retention in this chamber.
  • A substantial role for associative memory in the tested behavior.
  • Fewer counted sweeps than continuous learning at this operating point.
Still open

Broader capability and theory

  • Transfer to unseen task families and general world-model capability.
  • Universal convergence or correctness from internal agreement alone.
  • A general energy, scaling or efficiency advantage.
  • A mathematical or empirical derivation of EFMW physics.
